    Try this
    1. Predict. Halve the flying height at Cambuskenneth. Write down what you expect for the image resolution, the number of photos and the flight time, then try it.
    2. Investigate. Choose Abbey Craig with a constant height of 80 m above take-off. Where does the drone come too close to the ground? Look at the map and the profile, then switch to terrain following.
    3. Investigate. At Abbey Craig, how does the side overlap over the crag compare with the overlap you set? What would that mean for the finished map?
    4. Modify. What is the finest pixel size you can reach at Cambuskenneth on a single battery? Which other settings did you have to give up for it?
    5. Make. Plan a survey to map riverbank erosion at Cambuskenneth twice a year. Justify your image resolution, overlaps and direction of flight lines in a short paragraph.
    How the numbers are worked out
    • Image resolution. Ground sampling distance (GSD) = height above ground × sensor width ÷ (focal length × image width). Double the height and each pixel covers twice as much ground.
    • Footprint. One photo covers height × sensor width ÷ focal length across the flight line, and height × sensor height ÷ focal length along it.
    • Spacing. Flight lines sit one footprint width × (1 minus side overlap) apart. Photos are taken every footprint length × (1 minus front overlap).
    • Motion blur. Blur in pixels = speed × shutter time ÷ GSD. Keep it below about half a pixel for sharp maps.
    • Terrain. With a constant height above take-off, the height above the ground changes with the terrain. Over high ground the pixels get smaller, the footprints shrink and the overlap falls. Over low ground the drone may climb above the legal limit.
    • Flight time. Distance along all lines and turns, plus the trip from and back to the take-off point, divided by the speed, with 6 s for each turn and time to climb and descend. Batteries are counted at 80% of their endurance.
    Before a real flight

    This page is a teaching tool. A real survey needs proper flight planning software, a site risk assessment, the landowner's permission and compliance with the CAA Drone Code. In the UK open category the drone must stay within 120 m of the ground and in sight of the pilot. Many sites, such as historic monuments, need extra permission.
